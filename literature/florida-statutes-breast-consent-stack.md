## 458.324 — breast-cancer alternatives

Chapter 458 Medical Practice. Born 1984. Current definition of "medically viable" means modes of treatment generally considered by the medical profession to be within current, acceptable standards.

Duty: a physician treating a patient who is, or is judged at high risk of being, diagnosed with breast cancer shall inform, describe, and explain advantages, disadvantages, and risks of those alternatives enough for a prudent decision. Oral communication is allowed. The physician may recommend any mode judged best, without restriction. The chart must note compliance or noncompliance with that conversation.

This section does not reduce other informed-consent law.

House reading: this is a description duty for each medical egg. It is not a duty to treat a spice or homeopathic protocol as an equal alternative. C-033. S-018.

## 766.103 — Medical Consent Law

Chapter 766 Medical Malpractice. Defense rule for treating, examining, or operating without informed consent.

No recovery against listed providers when consent was obtained per the accepted standard in that medical community and a reasonable individual would have a general understanding of (1) the procedure, (2) medically acceptable alternative procedures or treatments, and (3) substantial risks and hazards recognized in that community — or when the patient would reasonably have undergone the procedure anyway.

Written consent that meets those tests, validly signed by a mentally and physically competent person, raises a rebuttable presumption of valid consent.

House reading: this is the Approval Engine test for one named procedure. A surgery form is not a radiation form. C-034. S-019.

## 381.026 — Patient's Bill of Rights and Responsibilities

Chapter 381 Public Health. Patient-facing list. A facility or provider may not require a waiver of these rights as a condition of treatment. This section shall not be used for any purpose in any civil or administrative action and neither expands nor limits other rights or remedies.

Rights that this house uses: dignity; prompt reasonable answers; a person of her choosing in consults unless safety blocks it; names and qualifications of providers; diagnosis, planned course, alternatives, risks, and prognosis (she may refuse that information); refuse any treatment, with the refusal documented; grievance path; impartial access; experimental-research consent; access to complementary or alternative care only when both she and a practitioner judge it in her best interest, in accordance with 456.41.

Responsibilities this house uses: accurate complete health information; say whether she comprehends the plan; own the consequences of refusal.

House reading: communication and refuse-and-document layer. Not a lawsuit engine. C-035 C-036. S-020.

## 456.41 — complementary or alternative treatments

Binds a practitioner who offers complementary or alternative care: explain nature, benefits, risks, and that practitioner's credentials for it; chart how the talk happened. Does not change scope of practice.

House reading: not a duty on a breast surgeon to offer clove oil or homeopathy. Two judgments required. C-037. S-021.
