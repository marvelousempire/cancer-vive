# Master Cancer Detriment Matrix

This matrix is the front-door comparison table for substances and research variables filed in Cancer Vive.

**Detriment to cancer** means evidence that a factor may damage cancer cells, suppress a cancer-relevant pathway, inhibit growth or survival, alter the tumor environment unfavorably for cancer, improve treatment response, or otherwise work against a cancer process.

That phrase does **not** mean the factor is proven to treat cancer in people. The matrix keeps four questions separate:

1. Is there a plausible cancer-relevant mechanism?
2. Is there preclinical evidence in cells or animals?
3. Is there human oncology evidence?
4. Is it an established cancer treatment?

A mechanism is not a clinical outcome. A trial is not proof of benefit. A food, extract, oil, drug, and purified compound are separate forms and do not inherit one another's evidence.

## Evidence-state key

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Human oncology evidence** | Human cancer studies or trials exist for the named form/context |
| **Preclinical signal** | Cell, animal, or pathway evidence relevant to cancer exists in the current corpus |
| **Indirect human evidence** | Human evidence exists, but not for treating cancer |
| **Not established** | Current Cancer Vive corpus does not establish the item as a human cancer treatment |
| **Harm concern** | The named form/context has a safety concern that matters independently of anticancer claims |
| **Needs source audit** | The item is filed, but Cancer Vive still needs paper-level cancer evidence entered into the source ledger |

## Operating rule

A row moves toward **human cancer detriment shown** only when the evidence itself moves:

```text
idea / traditional claim
→ defined substance and form
→ plausible mechanism
→ replicated cell evidence
→ animal evidence
→ human safety / pharmacology
→ human oncology signal
→ controlled human outcome evidence
→ established treatment only if the clinical evidence and authorities support that conclusion
```

No row skips levels because another form of the same substance looked promising.
